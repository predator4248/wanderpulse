Air Transit into I Gusti Ngurah Rai (DPS)

Bali’s main international gateway with modern terminals and rapid customs

I Gusti Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) is located in Tuban, just 15 minutes south of Kuta and 45 minutes from Ubud. It accommodates wide-body aircraft from major international hubs across the Americas, Europe, Australia, India, and East Asia.

1. Direct Non-Stop Corridors

Direct daily non-stops from Sydney (6h 15m), Melbourne (6h), Singapore (2h 45m), Kuala Lumpur (3h), Delhi/Mumbai (6h 50m), and Tokyo (7h 30m).

2. One-Stop Global Layover Hubs

From North America and Europe, premier one-stop flights operate via Singapore (Changi), Doha (Hamad), Dubai (DXB), or Jakarta (CGK).

3. Arrival, Visa on Arrival & Airport Shuttles

Complete e-VoA and electronic customs (ECD) before boarding. Official Grab & Gojek Lounge and prepaid airport taxi counters are situated immediately outside the arrivals hall.

The Scenic Trans-Java Rail & Strait Ferry

An unforgettable overland journey across volcanic Java into Bali

While Bali does not have an internal railway system, travelers can undertake one of Southeast Asia’s most iconic scenic rail journeys: taking the Indonesian Railway (KAI) Executive Express trains across Java to Ketapang Station, followed by a panoramic 45-minute ferry crossing across the Bali Strait into Gilimanuk.

1. Board Executive Rail in Jakarta / Surabaya

Board the Blambangan Ekspres or Argo Semeru. Features panoramic reclining seats, air conditioning, power sockets, dining car, and views of Mount Semeru and rice paddies.

2. Arrive at Ketapang Station (Banyuwangi)

The train terminates just 200 meters from Ketapang Harbor pier. Walk straight onto the Ro-Ro passenger ferry.

3. 45-Minute Strait Ferry into Bali

Ferries depart every 20 minutes 24/7. Cross the turquoise strait to Gilimanuk Harbor in West Bali, where tourist shuttles and private cars connect to Ubud and Denpasar.

Inter-City Sleeper Coaches & Island Shuttles

Direct air-conditioned luxury sleeper buses with ferry crossing included

Direct luxury executive sleeper coaches run from Jakarta, Bandung, Yogyakarta, and Surabaya directly into Mengwi Terminal in Bali. The bus rolls directly onto the ferry at Ketapang without requiring you to change vehicles or carry your luggage across!

1. Luxury Sleeper Coach Operators

Pahala Kencana, Damri, Lorena, and Tiara Mas operate premium double-decker coaches with flat sleeper bunks, individual entertainment tablets, and warm buffet meal stops.

2. Direct Roll-On / Roll-Off Ferry

You remain relaxed while the bus drives onto the sea vessel, allowing you to walk onto the ferry sundeck for sea breeze views during crossing.

3. Perama & Kura-Kura Local Island Shuttles

For traveling between Bali towns (Ubud, Canggu, Sanur, Amed, Lovina), Perama Tourist Shuttle provides punctual fixed-schedule transfers from $5–$12.

Marine Express Speedboats & Straits Ferries

Rapid ocean crossings to Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, Gili Islands & Lombok

Bali is the oceanic epicenter of the Lesser Sunda Islands. Thanks to the newly opened multi-million dollar Sanur New Harbor terminal, travelers enjoy dry, wave-free boarding directly onto modern air-conditioned catamarans and high-speed quad-engine passenger cruisers.

1. Sanur New Terminal to Nusa Islands (30–45 Mins)

Departing every 30 minutes from Sanur's modern jetty to Toyapakeh / Buyuk Harbor (Nusa Penida) and Jungutbatu (Nusa Lembongan). No wet feet, electronic boarding gates, and spacious luggage stowage.

2. Padang Bai Express to Gili Islands & Lombok (90 Mins)

High-speed twin-hull catamarans operated by Eka Jaya and Golden Queen glide across the Lombok Strait directly to Gili Trawangan, Gili Air, and Bangsal harbor in Lombok.

3. Luxury Serangan Catamaran & Kusamba Express

Serangan Marina offers premium direct routes with hotel lounge pickup, while Kusamba Port in East Bali delivers the fastest 20-minute jump straight into North Nusa Penida.

Vital Travel Knowledge

Essential Bali Road & Transit Survival Guide

Crucial real-world traffic laws, digital police enforcement, local taxi cartel rules, and navigating island shortcuts safely.

🚗 Law No. 22/2009

Drive on the Left & Narrow Road Etiquette

Traffic flows on the left side of the road with the driver seated on the right. Village roads (Gang) can be extremely narrow with deep irrigation drainage gutters on both sides. If meeting another car head-on, the vehicle with the nearest pull-out space yields.

💡 Tap your horn gently once when approaching blind cliff corners or mountain curves.
🪪 Mandatory Legal Document

International Driving Permit (IDP 1949)

Foreigners driving a scooter or car in Indonesia MUST carry an International Driving Permit (1949 Geneva Convention format) with their original national driver's license. Riding without an IDP will result in police fines and voids your travel medical insurance!

💡 Carry physical paper copies of your passport and IDP in your scooter underseat storage.
🪖 ETLE AI Camera Fine

Mandatory Helmets & Automated Traffic Cameras

Helmets with secured chinstraps (SNI approved) are strictly mandatory for both driver and passenger. The Bali Regional Police has deployed Electronic Traffic Law Enforcement (ETLE) AI cameras at major intersections on Sunset Road, Canggu, and Denpasar detecting unfastened helmets.

💡 WanderPulse rental scooters include 2 sanitized, high-grade certified helmets with visors.
🛑 Local Cartel Zones

Decoded: "No Grab / Gojek" Zones (Zona Pangkalan)

In central Ubud, Canggu Batu Bolong, Padang Bai harbor, and Uluwatu cliffs, traditional local village taxi associations (pangkalan) enforce exclusive passenger pick-up zones. Key secret: Grab/Gojek can ALWAYS drop you off anywhere! For pick-ups in red zones, walk 200m to the main bypass road, or book a private chauffeur.

💡 Dedicated private drivers with green plates are 100% exempt and welcome everywhere.
🌊 Jalan Tol Bali Mandara

Bali Mandara Overwater Toll Road

A 12.7 km engineering wonder built directly over the ocean connecting Ngurah Rai Airport, Benoa Harbor, and Nusa Dua. Bypasses South Bali's worst traffic gridlock in 8 minutes. Features a dedicated, barricaded motorcycle lane with speed limit 40 km/h and breathtaking ocean vistas.

💡 100% cashless toll. Requires an Indonesian e-Money (Mandiri) or Flazz (BCA) RFID card.
⛽ Pertamina vs Pertamini

Fueling Up: Official SPBU vs Roadside Glass Bottles

Official government gas stations (SPBU Pertamina) offer genuine Pertamax 92 octane fuel at regulated prices (~13,000 IDR/L). In remote villages, roadside wooden kiosks sell fuel in glass Absolute vodka bottles ("Pertamini"). Only use roadside bottles in emergencies as fuel can be diluted.

💡 Fill up your scooter tank at official Pertamina stations (red/blue signs) for optimal engine power.

Protection & Awareness

Tourist Scam Shield & 1-Tap Emergency SOS

Essential street wisdom to protect against rigged money changers, taxi imitations, monkey theft, and ocean rip-currents.

💵

Authorized Money Changers

Never use small back-alley kiosks with suspiciously high rates. Only exchange at certified counters displaying the green PVA Berizin (Bank Indonesia) seal like BMC Money Changer or Central Kuta with zero commission and electronic counting.

🚖

Authentic Bluebird Taxis

Look for the legitimate Bluebird Group logo on the roof light and windshield, driver ID on the dashboard, and a working meter. Alternatively, hail verified rides directly through the Grab or Gojek mobile applications.

🐒

Macaque Theft Prevention

At Uluwatu Temple and Ubud Monkey Forest, monkeys are trained to snatch sunglasses, loose phones, hats, and shiny earrings to barter for fruit. Secure all valuables inside zipped bags before entering.

🌊

Ocean Rip-Current Safety

South and West Bali beaches (Echo Beach, Canggu, Kelingking) have severe undertows. Strictly swim between red & yellow flags. At Kelingking Beach, never swim in the shorebreak due to extreme rogue dumpers.

Smart Island Travel

Essential Travel Guide & Cultural Etiquette

Local wisdom, climate seasonality, temple etiquette, and customs to ensure your Bali journey is seamless and respectful.

Best Season to Visit

Dry Season (May – October) brings clear blue skies, low humidity, and prime surf conditions. Green Season (November – April) brings refreshing tropical afternoon showers, lush green rice paddies, and lower accommodation rates.

Visa on Arrival & Tax

90+ nationalities qualify for a 30-Day Visa on Arrival (VoA) costing IDR 500,000 (~$32 USD), extendable once. Bali also implements a mandatory IDR 150,000 (~$10 USD) Sustainable Tourism Levy payable via the LoveBali portal.

Sacred Temple Dress

When entering holy temples (Pura), shoulders must be covered, and both men and women must wear a traditional sarong (Kamben) with a sash tied around the waist. Avoid stepping on woven morning offerings (Canang Sari) on sidewalks.

Cash & QR Payments

The Indonesian Rupiah (IDR) is the legal tender. Credit cards are accepted in hotels and restaurants, while cash is essential for street warungs and entry tickets. Local QRIS mobile payment is widely available everywhere.
